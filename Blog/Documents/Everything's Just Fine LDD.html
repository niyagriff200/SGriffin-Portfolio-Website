Gametype

Engine: Minecraft

Genre: Adventure/Mystery

Mode: Single-player (can be played as multiplayer)

Audience: 12+. Geared towards fan who like The Stanely Parable or The Truman Show or the Matrix.

Premise:  The player follows the same routine every day: wake up, eat breakfast, help neighbors, sleep. However, over time some inconsistencies start appearing, and hints that the perfectly normal neighborhood players were enjoying was not as normal as it seemed.

Story

Player:  The player is a long-time resident of Trumanville, who is always greeted warmly by their neighbors. They spend their time completing tasks for those neighbors whether that is walking their dog, finding their keys, or making a cake.

Timeline: This is the first playable level in Everything’s Just Fine.

Environment History: The town used to be a research facility that studied behavioral routines. However, it is now a well-polished neighborhood filled with wonderful scientists people.

Player Motivation: The player wants to follow their same routine, but they start to notice some contradictions that make them realize that something is off, which will take them on the adventure of a lifetime and finally leaving the neighborhood to find the hidden main facility.

Obstacles: The neighbors will try to distract and stop the player from leaving the neighborhood.

Reveals: The level will hint at things being a simulation, and the player will occasionally, see what appears to be a backstage, or a mysterious person following them while they’re doing their routine.

Gameplay

Main Objective: Complete a daily list of tasks assigned by various neighbors, which includes scavenger hunts, mini puzzles, and small errands. As the player progresses, inconsistencies start to appear.

Critical Path (Example):

1.      The player wakes up and goes to the kitchen to eat breakfast. They see a note left in the kitchen from their friend, telling them to meet them at the cherry blossom tree after sunset.

2.      On the way to the cherry blossom tree at the edge of the neighborhood, neighbors are giving the player tasks to complete

a.       Neighbor A: Find a kid playing hide and seek

b.      Neighbor B: Bake a cake (find ingredients first)

c.       Neighbor C: Go to store to buy flowers

d.      Optional Tasks: Attending Neighborhood meeting, explore (restricted?) areas.

3.      Players finally reaches the cherry blossom tree, but their friend is not there. However, they find a strange lever that opens the ground and leads to an underground train station (next level).

Map Size:  There will be 12 buildings the player can go explore. This includes their own home, neighbors’ home, the store, and town hall. There will be other buildings and structures, however they will be for different levels and background purposes.

Estimated Time: The estimated time it should take the player to complete the level is 30 minutes, however if they decide to do any of the optional tasks it should take them around 40 minutes to complete.

Difficulty: The difficulty should be easy, even to those who may be unfamiliar with Minecraft, however the optional tasks may only be easy to a Minecraft player (using trap door to vent) though it shouldn’t be too hard to figure out.

Skills Required:

·       Basic Navigation (walking, jumping, crouching)

·       Item Interaction (opening chests, opening doors, etc.)

·       Observation (to find the inconsistencies)

Skills Gained:

·       Interpretation of Inconsistencies

·       Recognizing Indirect Storytelling

Mechanics Introduced:

·       Scripted NPC routines and dialogue

·       Time of Day progression (day will be longer than 10 minutes)

·       Command Block Triggers

·       Player Communication (if played by multiple players)

Player Takeaway: A feeling of a fun routine, turned into a shocking realization that the neighborhood is just an experiment to study their behavior. Players will finish the level feeling curious, suspicious, and motivated to continue finding out more about the game.

Visuals/Effects/Audio

Visual Style:

·       Classic Minecraft suburban neighborhood built with existing blocks (i.e., quartz, oak, concrete)

·       Uses warm pastel materials to create an inviting feel

·       Symmetry and repetitive in building design to hint at the artificial perfection

·       Decorative elements, like paintings, banners, and flower pots

Time of Day & Weather:

·       Time locked to bright daylight using command blocks (always sunny, clear skies)

·       No rain or storms, unless for dramatic effect (or a sense of change, something not artificial for the end)

Lighting Effects:

·       Vanilla Minecraft lighting (torches, glowstone, etc.) to maintain visibility and a cozy feel

·       Redstone lamps to draw attention to certain (restricted?) areas

Audio:

·       Minecraft’s default ambient sounds

·       Potential for other sounds to cue NPC dialogue or to represent a change in feel using note blocks and jukeboxes.

Visual Storytelling Elements:

·       Signs with slogans like “Routine is Happiness” and/or “Have a Perfect Day!” (potentially custom, but will only do if time allows)

·       NPC routine loops via command blocks

·       Free and/or made skins to represent scientists, observers, and actors neighbors

·       Giant cherry blossom tree to serve as a visual anchor

·       Hidden underground area (train station) revealed using pistons for cinematic moment

Asset List

I will use some free Minecraft skins and if time allows some custom skins as well.

No other outside assets needed.

Feature List

All mechanics will be created using Minecraft’s built-in command block and redstone systems.

Gameplay Mechanics:

·        Command block-driven scavenger hunt triggers

·        NPC routine scripting via /tp, /say, or scoreboard logic

·        Daylight cycle manipulation (extended day, sunset arrival)

·        Puzzle interaction: item detection, lever activation, and environmental hints

·        Trapdoor-based traversal for venting/hidden areas

Narrative Mechanics:

·        Dialogue sequencing using signs, written books, or /tellraw

·        Environmental storytelling via item placement and signage

·        Visual cues hinting at backstage access or simulation cracks

·        Lever and piston-based cinematic moment at the cherry blossom tree

Progression Systems:

·        Task completion via checklist logic (books or visual prompts)

·        Optional discovery paths leading to hidden lore or restricted areas

·        Triggered level end condition: reaching underground station opens new chapter

Multiplayer (Optional):

·        Puzzle mechanics designed to support split tasks between players

·        Communication-based goals (e.g. one player discovers code, relays to another)

·        No dedicated server-side plugins required—can run through Minehut or Aternos
